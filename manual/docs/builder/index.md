# The Community Builder

The province is one world; the **Community Builder** makes others. **Build** in the top bar opens it, and the
simulation waits behind it, paused and unchanged, until you run a world or go **Back to the simulation**.

## Starting a world

The first time, the builder opens on **Build a community**, a choice of where to start:

| Start from | What you get |
|---|---|
| **Blank canvas** | An empty world, 1,600 × 1,200 m, with one city and one settlement, to build from the ground up. |
| **Masakhane, a small town** | About forty households in a village, a township and a hillside estate around a civic centre, to build on. |
| **Unity Province** | The built-in province itself, every building as drawn, to customise. |
| **Your worlds** | A world you saved, with its file, its buildings and houses, when it was saved and how many checks it has to fix. **Delete** removes one; a second click, **Delete it for good**, confirms. |
| **The world running now** | The built world the simulation is running, to change it (shown when one is). |

After that the builder reopens on the world you left, even one you never saved: it keeps a draft of your work on
this machine. **New / open** in the builder's top bar brings the choice back, and **Open .sce** beside it opens a
world someone shared with you, as a file.

## Laying it out

The **library** on the left holds everything a world is made of, with a search box at the top:

- **Sets**: a **street of houses**, a **village core**, a **civic centre** and the **airfield**.
- **Roads & areas**: a **Street** (7 m), an **Avenue** (9 m) and a **Highway** (11 m); the **Hyperline guideway**; a
  **Settlement** and a **City**.
- **Homes**: a small house, a house and a large house, each on a plot of its own size.
- **Faith & community**: the church (small, medium or large), the community hall, the library and the cemetery.
- **Learning**: the school.
- **Health**: the clinic, the medical centre, the hospital and the ambulance station.
- **Safety & government**: the police station, the magistrate's court, the council chamber, the post office and pay
  point, and Government House.
- **Shops & banks**: the spaza shop, the general dealer, the deli, the pharmacy, the mall, a Mutual Bank branch, a
  commercial bank and the reserve bank.
- **Work**: co-op offices, corporate towers, professional chambers, the workshop and the farm.
- **Parks & leisure**: the park and sports field, the square, the grand park, the sports centre, the stadium and the
  concert hall.
- **Transport**: the taxi rank, the bus stop, the bus and taxi terminus and the Hyperline station.

Each shows its footprint in metres, lays out its own rooms and furniture, and says what it does in the simulation.

- **Place** a building by picking it and clicking on the map. It turns its door to the nearest road and steps back
  to the road's building line, as the province's buildings do; <kbd>F</kbd> turns it, and holding <kbd>Alt</kbd>
  places it freely. It stays in hand, so each click places another; <kbd>Esc</kbd> or a double-click puts it down.
  A building that would overlap another or stand off the map is not placed.
- **Sets** lay out several at once. A **street of houses** is a road you drag, with plots along it: small, standard or
  large plots, on both sides, the north (or west) side or the south (or east) side. The **village core** is a high
  street with a church, school, clinic, police station, general dealer and Mutual Bank on one side, and a sports
  field, hall, cemetery, workshop, farm, co-op offices, taxi rank and bus stop on the other. The **civic centre** is a
  council chamber, medical centre, magistrate's court, commercial bank and square either side of a street. The
  **airfield** is the terminal and apron, control tower, hangar, fire and rescue and a 1.6 km runway (560 × 1,600 m);
  connect its Airport Road to the network.
- **Roads** are drawn by dragging. They run north to south or east to west, and their ends snap to the roads they
  meet. Select a road to change its width, or drag its ends.
- **Settlements** and **cities** are zones you drag out. Select one to drag its corners and sides.
- The **Hyperline's guideway** is drawn bend by bend, a click at each; every stretch runs north to south or east to
  west. <kbd>Enter</kbd> or a double-click ends it. Stations stand beside it, and two stations or more run trains.
- **Erase** removes a building, a road or a bend of the guideway with a click.
- **Select** anything to change it on the right: its name, its settlement, the side its door faces, its position and
  size, its options (a church's size, a police station's cells, a bank's head office) and what it will do in the
  simulation (**In the simulation**: what it serves as, the posts it fills first and where they are hired from, who
  pays for it, and the books it opens with). **To the road** puts a building's door back to the nearest road. Move,
  resize (within the asset's bounds; houses keep their plots), turn, duplicate and delete, one at a time or several:
  <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click adds to the selection, and a drag on open ground selects everything inside it. With several
  selected, **Move all to** puts them in one settlement. Undo and redo every step; snap to a 1, 2, 5 or 10 m grid
  (2 m at first).

The layers button on the tool rail chooses what the board shows: **Cities and settlements**, **Names**, **Rooms
(close up)**, **Grid** and **Marks on problems**, and the snap step.

## The people

A settlement's **Wealth** decides how its households are drawn: schooling, cars, cover, savings and work, from the
scenario's tiers. There are six residential tiers, from **the estates** through affluent, comfortable, middle-class
and working-class to low-income, and the **civic zone**, which seats no households. **Households at the start** sets
how many of its houses are lived in on day 0; four in five are, unless you say otherwise, and the rest wait for
newcomers.

Its **People here** settings set one settlement apart from its tier: **Schooling like** another tier's, **Without
work**, **Households with a car**, **Medical aid**, **Funeral cover**, **Life cover** and **Savings (months)**, as a
range. ↺ takes a setting back to the tier's.

A settlement uses its own church, shop, bank branch, park and bus stop (each house its nearest, where there are
several); a city shares its school, clinic, police, court, council, medical centre, cemetery, hall, taxi rank,
workshop, offices and farm. The rest, from the hospital and Government House to the stadium, the terminus and the
airport, serve the whole world. Every building brings the posts it cannot do without (a pastor for a church, a
doctor and two nurses for a clinic, three teachers for a school), filled first at the start, and the businesses
among them open books of their own.

## Cities and the world

Select a city to name it and set its **Kind**: a city of settlements, which elects a council of six with its medical
officer; a shared centre, with no council; or the airport. Its **Tagline** describes it, and **Churchgoing** is the
scenario's, or secular (6%), lukewarm (40%), churchgoing (75%) or devout (95%).

The **World** tab holds the world's own settings: its **Name**; its **Region**, which names the Mutual Bank and the
burial society; a **Description**; the **Map size**, from 400 to 12,000 m a side; and the **Baseline settlement**,
which seats the scenario's household count with its education mix and cover shares, as Ebenezer does in the
province (or none, and every settlement follows its tier). Under **At the start** it counts the buildings and houses,
the households, about how many people, the kilometres of road, the businesses keeping books, and the posts filled
first.

## Checks

The **Checks** tab lists what it finds in three groups. **Stops the world from running**: no homes, no shop, no
roads, a door more than 60 m from a road, roads that do not join the rest, a road at an angle, buildings
overlapping or off the map. **Will run, but**: what a missing place will mean (no clinic: the sick are nursed at
home; no school; no police station), a door far from its road, a road through a building, a Hyperline with fewer
than two stations. **Good to know**: choices the engine will make on its own (no church: a secular world; no road to
the edge of the map: newcomers arrive at the road end nearest it). Click a check to fly to it. A world with an error
cannot run until it is fixed.

## Running it

**Run this world** builds it and starts the simulation from day 0 on the scenario's assumptions. If a check stops
it, the builder opens the **Checks** tab instead. The Unity Province template, run unchanged, is the built-in
province.

![Masakhane running](../assets/img/built-world.webp)
<figcaption>The same town running: its first Sunday morning, 136 people in 38 households, the congregation in the pews and the rain over Ubuntu's high street.</figcaption>
</figure>

Everything else in the app works on it as on the province: the animated day and the 3D close-up, the analytics,
the books, the bank and SARS, the actuarial workbench, projections, Monte Carlo, the
[policy and stress lab](../analytics/lab.md) and the [exports](../exports/index.md). In the lab, *in Masakhane, on its
basis* runs every arm in the world on screen; unticked, the arms run in the built-in province on the defaults. The
world's own hash goes into the basis hash and the assumptions hash, so every export says which world it came from,
and runs pool only within one world. The app remembers the world it runs, and starts on it again, at day 0, the
next time.

The derivation is held to the province: deriving the province's own plan gives back the built-in province, table for
table and building for building, and a run through that path is the built-in run. The rules, and what a built world
assumes that the province does not, are in [Assumptions and provenance](../model/assumptions.md).

## Keeping worlds

A world is a **`.sce` file**, the project file of the Scelo family.

- **Save** (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd>) keeps it in the lab, in the `data/worlds` folder of the
  [app's folder](../reference/files.md) (**Help › Show Data Folder** opens it), listed under *Your worlds*. The file
  is named after the world, in lower case with hyphens for spaces (`masakhane.sce`). Saving again replaces the
  file, under its new name if you renamed the world. A world's `.sce` copied into that folder is listed too. The
  top bar says whether the world is **saved** or **not saved**.
- **Download .sce** gives you a copy to keep or share; **Open .sce** opens one.
- Scelo opens the same file as a project whose dataset is the world's buildings, one row each. A `.sce` saved by
  Scelo is a dataset, not a world, and the builder says so.

## Keys

| Keys | Does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>V</kbd> | Select |
| <kbd>H</kbd>, or <kbd>Space</kbd> held | Move the map |
| <kbd>R</kbd> | Road |
| <kbd>S</kbd> | Street of houses |
| <kbd>Z</kbd> | Settlement |
| <kbd>C</kbd> | City |
| <kbd>T</kbd> | Hyperline guideway (<kbd>Enter</kbd> ends it) |
| <kbd>X</kbd> | Erase |
| <kbd>F</kbd> | Turn the selection |
| <kbd>Del</kbd>, <kbd>Backspace</kbd> | Delete the selection |
| Arrows (<kbd>Shift</kbd>: ten steps) | Nudge the selection, or move the map |
| <kbd>G</kbd> | Snapping on or off |
| <kbd>0</kbd> | Fit the world |
| <kbd>=</kbd>, <kbd>−</kbd> | Zoom in and out |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> | Drop what is being drawn, then back to Select, then clear the selection |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd> / <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Y</kbd> (or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>Shift</kbd>+<kbd>Z</kbd>) | Undo / redo |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>D</kbd> | Duplicate the selection |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>A</kbd> | Select every building |
| <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd> | Save the world |

On a Mac, <kbd>Cmd</kbd> in place of <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>. With the mouse, the wheel zooms about the cursor, the middle or
right button drags the map, <kbd>Shift</kbd>+click adds to the selection, and a double-click stops placing or ends
the guideway.

While the builder is open it is the whole window: the province's keys and <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>1</kbd> /
<kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>2</kbd> wait until you leave it. Of the menus, **File › Save** (and **Save All**) saves the
world, and **Settings** and **Help › Keyboard Shortcuts** open over the builder; any other command says to leave the
builder first.
